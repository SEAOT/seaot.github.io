SEA OtTeRS

Extragalactic astronomy in time and redshift

Galaxies change on two very different clocks. Over billions of years, they grow, quench and merge — a story we read by looking further out in redshift. Over days to years, their active nuclei flicker — a story we read by watching the same object again and again. SEA OtTeRS works at the meeting point of these two axes, combining multi-epoch monitoring with deep multi-wavelength surveys — and builds the low-resolution spectrographs that put redshifts and line diagnostics within reach of Thailand’s own telescopes.

temporal

Reverberation mapping of active galactic nuclei

Photometric monitoring campaigns that measure the time lag between continuum and line emission, yielding black-hole masses and broad-line-region sizes for AGN well beyond the local universe.

redshift

Galaxy evolution and star-formation histories

Spectral energy distribution fitting and morphology of galaxies in deep fields, tracing the star-forming main sequence and its scatter across cosmic time.

temporal

Time-domain follow-up with the TNT

Rapid and scheduled observations with the 2.4-m Thai National Telescope and high-cadence instruments, contributing Thailand's longitude to global monitoring networks.

redshift

AGN hosts and the black hole–galaxy connection

Host-galaxy structure and environment of active galaxies, linking nuclear activity to the growth of the galaxy around it.

instrumentation

Low-resolution spectroscopy on Thai telescopes

Two in-house spectrographs — LRS for the 2.4-m TNT and CoLoRS for the 0.7-m TRT — designed for redshifts, classification and follow-up of variable and transient sources, built together with SEAL Lab.

temporal · redshift

AI-assisted analysis

Machine-learning tools for light-curve classification, SED inference and survey-scale data, so that monitoring and survey work scale with the data volume.

SEAL Lab

Precision engineering for instruments and experiments

Good data begins with good hardware. SEAL Lab develops the instrumentation and support technology behind observation: diamond-turned optics and mechanical parts, careful assembly and alignment, and laser metrology that verifies form, position and stability to the precision that optics demand.

machining

Diamond-turned optical components

Design and fabrication of mirrors, lens mounts and precision mechanical parts by single-point diamond turning, with surface form and roughness verified in-house.

metrology

Laser interferometry and displacement measurement

Laser-based measurement of surface form, straightness and positioning, supporting both fabrication tolerances and the calibration of telescope and instrument mechanisms.

assembly

Opto-mechanical assembly and alignment

Integration of optical subsystems in controlled conditions, from bench prototypes to instrument modules ready for the telescope.

support

Support technology for observation

Custom fixtures, test rigs and measurement tooling that keep instruments and telescopes performing to specification.
